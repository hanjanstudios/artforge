How it works

02 — 04

01

Upload the WIP

Drop in the piece wherever it's stuck — sketch, blocked-in painting, half-rigged model. Tag what kind of feedback you want.

02

Get forged

Matched reviewers leave structured, pinned annotations directly on the piece — not a comment thread you have to decode.

03

Revise in rounds

Push a new version, get a focused re-critique. Most pieces finish in three to five rounds.

04

Ship it

Mark it finished and it moves to your portfolio wall — proof, not just another folder of abandoned files.

What a critique looks like

03 — 03

1
Value structure

Light source reads clearly top-left, but the shadow side is losing form — push a mid-tone plane along the jaw before rendering detail.

2
Composition

Negative space top-right is inert. Either crop in 10% or add a secondary element to lead the eye back to center.

3
Intent

The hands are doing more storytelling than the face right now — lean into that, it's the strongest part of the piece.

Oil

Ashline Study No. 3

The midground kept swallowing the figure. Round two pushed a warm mid-tone plane behind the shoulder — the same fix noted in the critique example above — and the piece read clearly by round three.

Watercolor

Harbor, Unfinished

Started three years ago, restarted twice, abandoned each time at the same horizon line. A Forge Sprint group held the artist to a ten-day finish window; it shipped on day eight.

Concept Art

The Long Corridor

Composition critique flagged dead negative space at frame-right. One reviewer's pinned note — "give the eye somewhere to land" — became a single lamp, and the whole frame clicked into place.

Oil

Nocturne for a Small Room

Values were flattening under the artist's own reference photos. Structured critique separated light source from ambient fill, restoring the depth the sketch had promised.

Sculpture

Marrow & Rust

Photographed across four finishing passes. Each version critiqued against the last — not against an abstract ideal — until the surface finally matched the artist's original intent log.

Sketchbook

Field Notes 07

Never meant to be finished — until a reviewer asked what the loose gesture was actually for. The rationale log answered that question, and the sketch earned a frame.

Digital

Glasswing

Color critique called the palette muddy at 3 a.m. and clean by daylight. The fix wasn't more saturation — it was less: two hues cut, one pushed cooler.

Painting

Coastal Pattern Language

Four rounds of edge-control notes — hard where the rock meets water, soft everywhere else — turned a flat study into the piece now hanging in the artist's first show.

Digital

Interior, Late Light

The artist's own intent log said "quiet." The first critique round said the composition was shouting. Cropping in ten percent, exactly as the notes suggested, resolved both.

3D

Provisional Monument

Blocked for months on a base that never felt finished. A sprint group's blunt verdict — "the base is doing too much talking" — became the note that finally unstuck it.
